Part 01 · Overview

Keep a sale and an operating plan both ready, and choose on 22 January.

Sleep Media is the one profitable business under a senior loan of $103.1M, and its search revenue fell 23% this year to August. The plan is one standstill to 31 March 2027, a sale process run by a banker, and an operating plan tested by people who did not write it. The readings that separate the paths arrive by 15 January, and on 22 January a rule fixed in advance picks one.

Do now

Reserve rights, check the cash at the bank, and offer the standstill by 4 December. None of it needs new money.

Decide on 22 January

Sell, take ownership and collect the cash, or take ownership and run an operating plan. The lenders sell unless owning beats the best offer by a clear margin.

What it is worth

About $15.1M to Comvest from a sale in March. About $17.5M to $21.7M from operating if search revenue holds, and about $9.1M if it keeps falling.

The base case rests on one assumption: the largest partner's guaranteed price renews for 2027. This year's deal runs through December, and no terms for 2027 have been seen. The packet of 10 November valued Sleep Media on earnings that include that price, and this one keeps to it. It is the largest single swing in the packet.

1 · Where are we?

Sleep Media earns, Sleep Care spends, and the loan is many times the value.

Sleep Media earns about $13M a year before shared costs. Sleep Care and head office spend about $17M, so the company as a whole loses money. The senior loan reaches $103.1M in December, and Comvest holds about 58.9% of it. A sale of Sleep Media would fetch about $30.2M on the base case.

2 · Why now?

Cash interest starts in December, and the loan's own tests follow by March.

Until 30 November interest is added to the loan. After that it falls due in cash, at about $0.9M a month. The largest partner's guaranteed price expires on 31 December. Tests of $2.0M of cash and $2.0M of earnings follow, and the company cannot pass them on its own forecast. It needs the lenders' patience, and patience can carry conditions.

3 · What happened to search?

Search revenue is down 23% this year, with the steepest falls in the winter.

Sleep Foundation's search revenue was $8.0M from January to August, against $10.4M a year earlier. The fall was about half in January, narrowed to 4% in April, and was about 17% in July and August. Revenue fell less than Google clicks, because more readers clicked through to a seller and each click earned more under the largest partner's guaranteed price.

A sale returns about $15.1M, and operating lands above or below it.

Owning and collecting returns about $9.5M. Operating returns about $17.5M if search revenue holds and about $9.1M if it keeps falling 15% a year. 5 · What decides it?

The choice turns on how fast search revenue falls after 2027.

The base case already starts 2027 with search revenue about 3% below the twelve months to August. Operating equals a sale if search then falls about 6% a year on today's team, or about 10% on a core of six people. The record is a fall of 23% to 28% a year.

6 · How do we choose?

The readings arrive by mid-January, and a fixed rule turns them into a path.

By 31 December the lenders know whether partner terms for 2027 are signed and whether click-outs held after Google's update. By 15 January they have written offers and a tested operating plan. On 22 January they sell unless owning beats the best offer by the greater of $1.0M and 10% of the value of owning. The rule is adopted now, before the offers arrive, so the choice on the day follows the evidence.

Part 02 · Situation

Sleep Media earns the money, Sleep Care spends it, and the loan's deadlines are close.

This part sets out what the company's own records show: the two businesses, the debt, the dates, the cash and the search revenue that Sleep Media lives on. Actual figures run to August 2026, and later months are management's forecast. The record is read against last year, and not against budget.

Sleep Media earns the money, and Sleep Care and head office spend more.

OneCare Media trades as Sleep Doctor and runs two businesses inside one borrower. Sleep Media publishes sleepfoundation.org and earns a commission when a reader clicks through to a seller. Sleep Care sells home sleep apnea tests and CPAP breathing machines, for cash and since 2026 through insurance.

What holds. Sleep Media earns about $13M before shared costs on management's forecast. Standing on its own, it earned about $8.7M over the twelve months to August.

What to watch. Sleep Care and head office together spend about $17M, so the company as a whole loses money.

The debt is many times what the business is worth.

The senior loan was $100.6M in September 2026 and reaches $103.1M in December as unpaid interest is added to it. Comvest holds about 58.9% of it, a claim of $60.7M. Falcon holds $85.9M of junior notes that rank behind the senior loan. On each case in this packet the value reaches the senior loan and nothing below it.

What holds. The senior lenders are the only parties with money still at stake, which gives them the standing to set terms.

What to watch. Comvest's share is from an April 2024 list, and the current list of lenders has not been seen.

The loan's own deadlines fall inside the nineteen weeks to closing.

Until 30 November 2026 interest is added to the loan in place of being paid. After that it falls due in cash, at about $0.9M a month. A test that the company holds $2.0M of cash runs monthly to 28 February 2027, and a test of $2.0M of earnings falls on 31 March 2027.

What holds. The company cannot pass these tests on its own forecast, so it needs the lenders' patience, and patience can carry conditions.

What to watch. The date of the first cash interest payment sits in loan documents that have not been seen.

Cash has fallen for two years and now rests on a domain sale.

The company forecasts $2.85M of cash for 27 November, of which $1.9M came from selling a web domain in January. Average cash was $10.1M in October 2024 and $3.2M in September 2026.

What holds. The company's forecast stays above the $2.0M test to the end of January, before any cost of change or any cash interest.

What to watch. Receipts missed the two latest forecasts, and no bank statement has been seen.

Search revenue fell by nearly a quarter this year, despite a higher price per click.

Sleep Foundation's search revenue was $8.0M from January to August 2026, against $10.4M a year earlier, a fall of 23%. Google clicks were 975K in October against 1,874K a year earlier. Revenue fell less than clicks, because a larger share of readers clicked through to a seller and each click earned more under the largest partner's guaranteed price.

What holds. The falls since March are smaller than in the winter: about half in January, and about 17% in July and August.

What to watch. April and May came within 7% of a year earlier, and the gap has widened again since June. The guaranteed price expires on 31 December, and management's forecast needs search revenue from September to December to run 33% above a year earlier.

Against last year, the third quarter fell where the company reports it steady.

The company's board pre-read reads the third quarter against a budget that was cut after May, and calls it back on plan. Against the same months of 2025, revenue that depends on Google fell from $4.83M to $3.48M, down 28%. All Sleep Media revenue fell 12%, from $4.97M to $4.36M.

What holds. Revenue that does not depend on Google, which is advertising, AI referrals, video and e-mail, rose from $0.14M to $0.88M.

What to watch. The two company sources do not fully reconcile, and neither is checked against partner statements.

The sale price is close to the earlier packet's, and the path has changed.

The packet of 10 November 2026 valued Sleep Media as a business before debt, over five years. This packet values what Comvest can collect, after costs, the other lenders and the wait. The base case keeps that packet's assumption that the partner's guaranteed price carries on, so the sale price is close to it. Part of the change is new information: actual results in place of a forecast, and a newer filing for the mark. Part is method: growth counts where a tested plan shows it, and the cost and new money of a takeover are counted.

What holds. The debt, the $5.6M cost of running Sleep Media on its own, the 15% rate and the range of 3.0 to 3.5 times earnings are unchanged. So is the weight on next year's partner terms, which the earlier packet called the top priority.

What to watch. The base case of $30.2M needs signed terms and click-outs holding near 32,000 a week. Neither reading is known yet, and without renewal the sale price is $14.8M.

Part 03 · Scenarios and logic

December readings pick the sale case, and a fixed rule picks the path.

This part sets out how value is built, how a price becomes cash to Comvest, the four sale cases and the severe case, and the three paths after decision day. Each sale case is a sale that closes on 31 March 2027. No odds and no blended value are shown, because the readings that pick the case arrive by 31 December.

Value is built from clicks to sellers, what each earns, cost and a buyer's price.

A click-out is a click from a Sleep Foundation page through to a seller, and it is the unit that earns commission. Search revenue is weekly click-outs times 52 times revenue per click-out. Revenue per click-out divides search revenue from every site by Sleep Foundation's click-outs, so the January to August figure of $5.73 is a little above the $5.40 that Sleep Foundation alone earned per click-out. The base case uses $7.02, the August figure under the partner's guaranteed price. Other revenue is added, the cost of running Sleep Media on its own is taken off, and a buyer pays a number of years of the earnings that result.

What holds. The model was built from two separate analyses of the same records, and it reproduces each of their values before any input is changed.

What to watch. No input rests on a bank statement, a signed loan document, a supplier's quote or an offer.

A price reaches the four lenders in proportion, and then Comvest.

On the base case a price of $30.2M leaves about $25.6M for the four lenders and about $15.1M for Comvest, in today's money. The price first pays the selling cost and the costs the old company leaves behind. Those leftover costs are $1.75M on the base case: the $0.76M cash gap at closing, which the new money has covered, and $1.0M of later costs for tax, priority claims and the wind-up. What is left is put in today's money, which means reduced for the wait at 15% a year. It goes to the four senior lenders in proportion to their share of the loan: Comvest 58.87%, Vistria Credit 26.76%, Cliffwater 9.3%, Pantheon 5.1%.

What holds. Keeping the money already owed to the company with the seller is worth about $0.7M to Comvest, for one line on the bid form.

What to watch. Offers are likely to include a part paid later. A quarter of the price paid that way costs Comvest about $2.1M on the base case.

The readings that pick the sale case arrive by 31 December.

Two questions pick the case. Are 2027 partner terms signed, in a form that carries over to a new owner, by 31 December? Do weekly click-outs hold at 27,000 or more after Google's update? A fall below 21,000 a week for two weeks is the severe case, in which a sale has no value to the lenders and the site is run off for cash.

What holds. Signed terms with lower click-outs return more to Comvest than unsigned terms with click-outs holding.

What to watch. Weekly click-outs were last matched for August, and no count for November has been seen.

The three paths differ in who owns Sleep Media, and for how long.

The work to 22 January is the same whatever happens next: a standstill, a sale process run by a banker, and an operating plan tested by people who did not write it. A sale ends the lenders' involvement in March. Ownership keeps it for up to two years and needs more new money first. The exhibit sets out what each path needs to be true, what the record says today, and when each point will be known.

What holds. The first 60 days are the same on each path, so nothing is given up by keeping all three open.

What to watch. The point that matters most for operating, the yearly fall in search revenue after 2027, is not known by decision day. The first review is on 30 September 2027.

A sale rests on public price evidence and on buyers not yet approached.

No offer has been seen, and buyers are approached from 14 December. The base case's price of 3.5 times next-year earnings is above what listed publishers and brokered sites fetch, on the view that a buyer pays more for signed partner terms. Listed publishers that depend on search trade at about 3 times earnings, and brokered content sites sell at 2.0 to 2.8 times annual profit. A 2026 broker guide gives 3 to 10 times annual earnings for private site sales, with content sites in the lower to middle part.

What holds. The likeliest buyer already owns five rival sites, so it knows the traffic risk and can price it.

What to watch. Buyers answer search exposure with a lower price, a part of the price tied to later results or an amount held back. A part paid later is valued at no more than half.

Operating rests on holding search with a smaller team and new revenue.

The plan protects what earns the money and changes cost by stages. The site's rankings rest on product testing and medical review, so those stay funded. AI tools are proved on about 50 pages before any role changes, and the team steps down to six over the first year. Six revenue ideas make up a target of $1.5M a year by late 2028, each run as a 30-day test with a budget and one owner.

What holds. Separating Sleep Media from head office removes far more cost than AI does, and that saving arrives on any path.

What to watch. A tenth of search revenue is worth more than the whole AI saving, so the roles that protect revenue are the last to change. The core of six is not quoted, and none of the six revenue ideas is contracted.

An offer has to beat what the lenders can show ownership would return.

The lenders sell unless owning beats the best offer by the greater of $1.0M and 10% of the value of owning. The minimum price is the offer below which they do better by owning, after that margin. If ownership means collecting cash from a shrinking business it is about $18.0M. If a tested plan shows search revenue holding it is about $31.4M, and about $34.8M with the team down to six.

What holds. A close call goes to the cash offer. An offer counts if it has proof of funds, a stated structure and a closing on or before 31 March.

What to watch. The operating plan is tested by the independent officer and the banker, who did not write it. Without a tested plan, ownership is valued as collecting cash.

Five stop rules end the path before losses build.

The path is a bet that search revenue can be held, and each rule names a reading that shows the bet is going wrong. The first review is on 30 September 2027. If search keeps falling and the lenders stop there, Comvest gets back about $9.6M to $11.5M, against $9.1M by carrying on.

  • Click-outs collapse. Below 21,000 a week for two weeks: cost is cut to the run-off level, and the site is sold or run for cash.
  • Search keeps falling. Search revenue for a quarter more than 10% below the level the case assumed: the sale process starts within 30 days.
  • Cash. The new company's cash below $1.0M, or the new money not on track to be repaid by the end of 2028: the board reviews the budget and the path.
  • Partners. The largest partner gives notice, or cuts its price by more than a fifth: the sale process starts.
  • The plan. Neither the cost stages nor a revenue test has passed by the second review: the lenders go back to collecting cash and sell by mid-2028.

What holds. The business is kept ready for sale throughout, so stopping takes weeks and not months.

What to watch. Trying and stopping still costs about $3.6M to $5.5M against a sale in March, and about $6.7M if the fall is fast.

Part 06 · What it takes

Each path needs new money first, and most decisions fall before 11 December.

This part sets out the new money each path needs, the timetable, the decisions asked of Comvest with their dates, the conditions for operating, and what has not been checked at source.

Holding the cash floor to March needs about $2.8M of new money.

Cash falls from $2.85M on 30 November to below zero by 31 March on the base case, before any funding. Holding $2.0M at each month end needs about $2.8M. What to watch. The advance payment and the domain sale are requests and not receipts. Without a new owner for Sleep Care they leave the need at about $2.0M.

If the lenders own, they fund the costs a sale price would have paid.

In a sale, the price pays the selling cost and the costs the old company leaves behind. If the lenders take ownership there is no price, so they fund those costs, the cost of the transfer and the first weeks of the new company. That comes to about $3.8M before the levers, about $2M more than a sale uses.

What holds. The new company earns about $0.7M a month on the base case, so it is out of the red within three months and repays the lenders from its own cash.

What to watch. Comvest's share would be about $2.2M before the levers, and the money stays out until early 2028 if search holds.

A single route runs to decision day, and three paths follow it.

The standstill is signed on 11 December and runs to 31 March 2027. Buyers are approached from 14 December, written offers are due on 15 January, and decision day is 22 January. A sale then closes on 31 March, and ownership runs to a later sale by mid-2028 or by March 2029.

What holds. A missed date stops further funding or starts the fallback, so the timetable enforces itself.

What to watch. The approach to buyers runs across two holiday weeks. Each month added to a closing costs Comvest $0.4M to $0.6M where the terms do not renew, and the cost was not measured on the base case.

How a sale is valued

Weekly click-outs times 52 times revenue per click-out, plus other revenue, less the cost of running Sleep Media on its own, give earnings for the twelve months from closing. A buyer pays a number of years of those earnings. Selling cost and leftover costs come off, the result is put in today's money at 15% a year, and Comvest takes its 58.9% share.

How ownership is valued

Month by month from April 2027, the new company's earnings build its cash. Cash above $2.0M is paid to the lenders each month. The business is then sold at a stated number of years of the next twelve months' earnings. Each amount is put in today's money at 15% a year.

How the cases are set

Two readings select the sale case: whether 2027 partner terms are signed, and whether click-outs hold at 27,000 a week or more. No odds and no blended value are shown. The base case is the case with terms signed and click-outs holding, which is the reading the packet of 10 November took. The no-renewal view sets the base to the case with terms unsigned.

How the mark is compared

Each scenario is Comvest's share on the same claim of $60.7M, in today's money, after the new money is repaid first. The mark is the value shown on 30 September 2026. It is an accounting value at that date, and not a bid.

Where the models come from

The plan of 19 November printed the sale cases and ran ownership from the no-renewal case. For this packet the same ownership model was run again from the base case, and it reproduces the plan's printed row for that case. The sale model was built from two separate analyses of the same records. The ownership model was built for this work and matched to the sale model's values for owning and collecting, with three inputs set to make them agree. Neither has had an outside review.
